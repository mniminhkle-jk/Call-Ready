# A. O. Smith
Manufacturer · Milwaukee, WI · NYSE: AOS  
Stage: **Research** · Brief reviewed: 2026-10-17

Largest maker of water heaters in North America, plus boilers (Lochinvar) and water treatment; about 78% of sales in North America.

> **Sharpest Signal** A. O. Smith is closing its Groveport, OH water-treatment plant and its Lockbourne, OH warehouse by Dec 11, 2026, and moving that work to Appleton, WI and Haltom City, TX. Its water-treatment shipping lanes get redrawn over the next 60 days, including inventory transfers out of Ohio and new outbound lanes from Wisconsin and Texas. [4]
>
> *Why it gets the call:* A network move has a hard date, new origins and no carrier history on the new lanes. That's the moment a transportation team takes a call about capacity.

## Openers (CRVA)
- *RISK:* “I saw the Groveport water-treatment work is moving to Appleton and Haltom City by December. How are you covering the new outbound lanes while the move happens?”
- *PAIN:* “Your Q2 call mentioned customer pre-buy ahead of the price increase. When orders spike like that, where does truck capacity get tight first: the Tennessee plants or El Paso?”
- *GET:* “With Leonard Valve now part of A. O. Smith, are Cranston's shipments moving onto your carrier network or staying separate?”

## Questions: “I know ___ / I don't know ___”
- I know the Groveport operations move to Appleton and Haltom City by December 11. What I don't know is whether the inventory and equipment transfers are already covered.  
  Follow-ons: How many loads are you expecting out of Groveport and Lockbourne? → Who is planning the new Appleton and Haltom City outbound lanes? → What happens to dealer orders if a transfer load slips?
- I know your Traffic Department handles daily LTL pickups at Johnson City. What I don't know is how you decide when an LTL order should be a truckload or multi-stop.  
  Follow-ons: How often do you consolidate? → What does a missed pickup cost you with a wholesaler? → Who reviews carrier performance each quarter?
- I know Juárez opened a third plant and feeds your El Paso DC. What I don't know is how freight crosses from Juárez today and who handles it.  
  Follow-ons: How long do loads dwell at the border? → What changes if USMCA terms shift in the annual review? → Would a second cross-border option be useful?
