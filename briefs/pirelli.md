# Pirelli Tire North America
Manufacturer · Rome, GA (US HQ) · Milan, Italy (group) · BIT: PIRC  
Stage: **Research** · Brief reviewed: 2026-10-17

Premium car and motorcycle tire maker; US headquarters and plant in Rome, GA, with most US volume imported from Mexico, Europe and Brazil.

> **Sharpest Signal** On Sept 22, 2026 Pirelli's board approved about $1.2B to expand the Rome, GA plant to about 6M car tires a year by 2033, with capacity starting to rise from 2028. In 2025, Rome supplied only about 5% of US demand; the rest came from Silao, Mexico (55%) and Europe/Brazil (40%). Most US volume keeps crossing the border and coming through the ports for years while Rome ramps. [1][2][4]
>
> *Why it gets the call:* It's fresh, it's a public win to congratulate, and it opens a real question: how does logistics change over a multi-year ramp, while USMCA is under annual review?

## Freight profile
- **What they ship:** Finished car, SUV and motorcycle tires: light, bulky, high cube. ✓[1]
- **What they ship:** Equipment implied: 53' dry van and intermodal containers; tires cube out before they weigh out. ?inferred
- **Where:** 2025 US supply mix: about 5% Rome, GA; 55% Silao, Mexico (plant described as 'fully saturated'); 40% Europe and Brazil. ✓[4]
- **Where:** US warehousing run by Kuehne+Nagel in McDonough, GA and Rialto, CA (800,000 sq ft), where domestic shipments are consolidated. ✓[6]
- **Where:** Mexico-to-US flows have historically moved by rail through Texas and by truck; ocean imports come in through Savannah/Charleston and LA/Long Beach. ?inferred
- **How often / when:** Replacement-tire peaks: winter changeover sell-in (Aug–Nov) and spring. ?inferred
- **How often / when:** Construction and robotics equipment moving into Rome from 2027 as the expansion phases start. ?inferred
- **Who moves it today:** Uses 3PL Kuehne+Nagel for US warehousing and consolidation (partnership extended in 2022). ✓[6]
- **Who moves it today:** Logistics team is upgrading to third-generation WMS/TMS with barcode scanning and GPS shipment tracking (June 2025). ✓[7]
- **Who moves it today:** Revised logistics flows in 2025 to soften tariffs (15% on EU car tires; Mexico duty-free as USMCA-compliant). ✓[5]

## Company now
- Board approved a roughly €1B / $1.2B US investment plan on Sept 22, 2026, with phased work starting in 2027. ✓[1]
- About 1,000 new jobs; most hiring expected in 2029; full operations projected for 2033. Rome currently employs 234. ✓[2]
- Cyber Tyre (sensor-equipped connected tire) production started at Rome; announced May 2026. ✓[3]
- The US is about 40% of global High Value tire volume, Pirelli's core segment. ✓[1]
- Sinochem cut its stake from 34.1% to 20.1% in July 2026 after Italy's golden-power decree. ✓[9]

## Openers (CRVA)
- *RISK:* “Congratulations on the board approval for Rome last month. While capacity ramps toward 2033, how is your team planning the Silao and port flows that still cover most of US demand?”
- *PAIN:* “I saw your logistics team talk about moving to third-generation WMS with GPS tracking. Which outbound lanes from McDonough have been hardest to get reliable tracking on?”
- *GET:* “With winter-tire sell-in running now, where do you need the most extra dry-van capacity out of McDonough and Rialto?”

## Questions: “I know ___ / I don't know ___”
- I know Silao was described as fully saturated and supplies over half of your US demand. What I don't know is how those loads cross today: rail, truck, or a mix.  
  Follow-ons: What share goes by truck through Laredo? → Where does border dwell hurt you most? → What would a backup cross-border carrier need to prove to you first?
- I know Kuehne+Nagel runs your McDonough and Rialto warehouses. What I don't know is who chooses the outbound truckload carriers: you or K+N.  
  Follow-ons: How is that carrier mix reviewed? → When does winter sell-in push you over your routing guide? → Who would need to sign off on adding a carrier?
- I know the Rome expansion starts in 2027 and ramps capacity from 2028. What I don't know is whether Rome will ship more direct to OEMs and dealers or keep consolidating through McDonough.  
  Follow-ons: How will that change your lane plan? → Who is planning the network for 2028? → What would make a carrier useful to that plan now?
