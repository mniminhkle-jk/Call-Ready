# BlueLinx
Distributor · Atlanta, GA · NYSE: BXC  
Stage: **Research** · Brief reviewed: 2026-10-17

Two-step wholesale distributor of building products (lumber, panels, engineered wood, siding, millwork, outdoor living) serving all 50 states.

> **Sharpest Signal** In September 2026 BlueLinx brought three supplier lines into its network at once: Trex inventory landing at 11 Midwest and Southeast DCs, RDI railing added at 8 more DCs, and LP SmartSide stocked in 8 new markets in Texas, South Carolina and Florida. Its Q2 results already named third-party freight as one reason SG&A rose $12.1M. [2][3][4][1]
>
> *Why it gets the call:* New supplier lines mean new inbound lanes and replenishment cycles that nobody has carrier history on yet. A logistics lead is most open to a backup carrier right when lanes are new.

## Company now
- Q2 2026 net sales $814M, up 4.4% YoY; adjusted EBITDA up 33%. ✓[1]
- Q2 SG&A rose $12.1M, attributed to Disdero, fuel and third-party freight expenses, and employee costs. ✓[1]
- Q3 2026 outlook: average daily sales volumes higher than Q3 2025 and slightly up from Q2. ✓[1]
- Strategy is mix shift to specialty products (engineered wood, siding, millwork, outdoor living), now about 69% of sales. ✓[1]
- Customers: national home centers, pro dealers, co-ops, specialty distributors, local dealers, industrial manufacturers. ✓[1]

## Openers (CRVA)
- *RISK:* “I saw Trex inventory started landing at your 11 Midwest and Southeast DCs in September. How has the first replenishment cycle out of Winchester and Fernley gone?”
- *PAIN:* “Your Q2 release named third-party freight as part of the SG&A increase. Where is outside freight costing you the most right now: inbound from suppliers or transfers between DCs?”
- *GET:* “With LP SmartSide now stocked in seven Texas markets plus Charleston and Lake City, are those new stocking points fed from the mill or from your existing DCs?”

## Questions: “I know ___ / I don't know ___”
- I know you run your own fleet for dealer deliveries. What I don't know is how you split inbound supplier freight between your trucks and outside carriers.  
  Follow-ons: Which lanes go to outside carriers first when volume spikes? → How far ahead do you book flatbed capacity for spring? → What happens to dealer fill rates when a load gets dropped?
- I know Trex is now shipping into 11 of your DCs. What I don't know is whether Trex ships prepaid or you control that inbound freight.  
  Follow-ons: Who decides the carrier on those inbound loads? → How many loads a week do you expect per DC in peak season? → Who signs off on adding a new carrier for those lanes?
- I know spring is your peak season. What I don't know is how you secured flatbed and step-deck capacity last March through May.  
  Follow-ons: Where did you come up short? → What did a late load cost you with a pro dealer? → What would a reliable surge carrier be worth on your top three lanes?
